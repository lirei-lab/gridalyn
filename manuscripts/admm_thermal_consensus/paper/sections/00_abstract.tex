Decentralized coordination of flexible loads through the alternating direction
method of multipliers (ADMM) is an established way to flatten the winter peaks
that cold-climate electric heating imposes on distribution feeders. Two gaps
limit its practical credibility. First, multi-agent schemes are fragile to
communication failures: when agents stop responding, the coordinated aggregate
degrades in ways rarely quantified. Second, the coordinated aggregate is almost
never checked against the physics of the feeder it is supposed to relieve. We
close both gaps with a network-validated pipeline that combines a sharing-ADMM
coordinator, a LightGBM imputer that reconstructs the heating of non-responsive
agents, and an AC power-flow validation stage on a synthetic low-voltage
residential feeder of 74 all-electric homes behind a single 400~kVA MV/LV
distribution transformer, modeled in pandapower. On a synthetic
Trois-Rivi\`eres cold day, ideal coordination cuts the aggregate peak by 9.8\%,
from 401.6 to 362.3~kW, and drives the peak-to-average ratio from 1.109 to
1.000. On the feeder, coordination clears a 109.6\% transformer thermal overload
down to 98.5\% loading; low-voltage levels stay healthy ($\approx 0.96$~p.u.,
never approaching 0.95~p.u.), so the transformer is the binding constraint. A
finely-resolved sweep shows the cleared state holds on a plateau through a
non-responsive fraction of about 0.70 and then degrades as a roughly linear ramp
--- not a sudden step --- crossing the thermal limit near 0.75. A Monte-Carlo
analysis over which agents fail and the forecast error shows the violation onset
is probabilistic, centered near 0.72 (96\% of realizations breach by 0.75) and
certain by 0.80. The result is a fully reproducible study that ties coordination
quality to measurable feeder feasibility.
