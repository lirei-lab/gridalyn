Decentralized coordination of flexible loads through the alternating direction
method of multipliers (ADMM) is an established way to flatten the winter peaks
that cold-climate electric heating imposes on distribution feeders. Two gaps
limit its practical credibility. First, multi-agent schemes are fragile to
communication failures: when agents stop responding, the coordinated aggregate
degrades in ways rarely quantified. Second, the coordinated aggregate is almost
never checked against the physics of the feeder it is supposed to relieve. We
close both gaps with a network-validated pipeline that combines a sharing-ADMM
coordinator, a LightGBM imputer that reconstructs the heating of non-responsive
agents, and an AC power-flow validation stage on the IEEE 33-bus feeder modeled
in pandapower. On a synthetic Trois-Rivi\`eres cold day with 30 electric-heating
homes, ideal coordination cuts the aggregate peak by 12.7\%, from 169.1 to
147.7~kW, and drives the peak-to-average ratio from 1.146 to 1.001. On the
feeder, coordination clears a 115\% thermal line overload down to 99.7\% and
lifts the worst minimum voltage from 0.905 to 0.918~p.u. The cleared state is
resilient up to a non-responsive fraction of 0.6, then re-violates at 0.8, where
imputation error re-introduces a 108\% overload. A Monte-Carlo analysis over
which agents fail and the forecast error further shows the violation onset is
probabilistic rather than sharp, centered near a non-responsive fraction of 0.6
(56\% of realizations breach the thermal limit). The result is a reproducible,
governed study that ties coordination quality to measurable feeder feasibility.
